CrowbarOS · agent-first

Don't ship a desktop.
Ship the ability to make one.

An operating system that starts lean, where each person shapes their own OS with their agent. What you asked for is the source. Everything built from it can be undone, rebuilt with a better model, carried to a new machine, or shared.

The shift

People are increasingly building bespoke software instead of installing it. Today that's mostly developers working with agents. The cost of a small tool that fits one person exactly is falling fast, and within a few years most people will make software this way.

If software becomes something you describe instead of something you install, the thing you own is the description.

So the substrate matters most: a small, stable base, an agent that helps you work out what you want before it builds, safety that does not depend on the model being right, and a way to keep, undo, carry, and share what you make.

What it feels like

you › I write in the mornings. Give me a calm writing mode with no notifications until noon.
agent · two questions first: should anyone get through before noon? Weekends too?
you › Sam can always get through. Weekdays only.
agent · intent saved with 3 checks you confirmed · built "Morning Pages" and a notification rule · applied live, one step to undo
you › (points at the page list) Sort these by date.
agent · intent updated, checks still pass

No app store browsing, no settings archaeology, no terminal required. Everything that stays on your machines can be undone, so the agent just does it. Anything that leaves them, spends money, or acts as you needs your say.

Seven first principles

01

Intent is the source

Your intents, with checks you confirmed, are your OS. The Crowbar State is a pinned lockfile built from them, and a better model can re-derive it as a diff you review.

02

Three layers

Base changes rarely, at boot. The Environment, where Tools live, changes live with instant undo. Your Data is never rolled back with them.

03

Deterministic safety

Handles, labels, and one undo boundary around your fleet. Inside it, things just happen. Crossing it needs a grant, a rule, or you. No model on the trust path.

04

Tools are living and disposable

Generated Tools are capability-safe components with UI the system draws. Point at any part and say what to change.

05

The agent is a role

The OS owns the role and its authority. The harness and model are swappable, from open weights on your own hardware to Grok Bot, and none of them is trusted.

06

The owner is sovereign

You can always take full control. Lean by default, as heavy as you choose. Recovery never depends on a model.

07

Portable and shareable

One OS across your fleet, with encrypted sync. Share your intents; a friend's agent rebuilds them, and no authority transfers.

The stack: invent the top, borrow the bottom

Nothing about agent-first needs a new way to talk to a Wi-Fi chip, and drivers are what kill new operating systems. Isolation lives in the component runtime and the capability broker, so the kernel is a replaceable driver host. We choose Linux for its drivers.

The hard problems still open

  1. Checks for everyone. How do people who aren't developers confirm checks precise enough to catch a bad re-derive?
  2. Live apply. How do we swap a running Tool atomically without losing its state?
  3. The UI vocabulary. How rich must declarative UI be before generated Tools stop needing native windows?
  4. Data newer than code. What happens when the Environment rolls back but your Data doesn't?
  5. The Allowance. What should back the money you give your agent: a capped card, an agent wallet, or a payment protocol?
