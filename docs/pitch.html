CrowbarOS · agent-first

Don't ship a desktop.
Ship the ability to make one.

An operating system that starts lean, where each person shapes their own OS with their agent. What you asked for, the checks you confirmed, and the build you accepted are the source. Changes to your tools are checked in a private copy before they go live, and can be undone, rebuilt with a better model, carried to a new machine, or shared.

The shift

People are increasingly building bespoke software instead of installing it. Today that's mostly developers working with agents. The cost of a small tool that fits one person exactly is falling fast, and within a few years most people will make software this way.

If software becomes something you describe instead of something you install, the thing you own is the description.

So the substrate matters most: a small, stable base, an agent that helps you work out what you want before it builds, security that comes first and does not depend on the model being right, and a way to keep, undo, carry, and share what you make.

What it feels like

you › I write in the mornings. Give me a calm writing mode with no notifications until noon.
agent · two questions first: should anyone get through before noon? Weekends too?
you › Sam can always get through. Weekdays only.
agent · intent saved with 3 checks you confirmed · built "Morning Pages" and a notification rule in a fork · here is a preview
you › (confirms)
agent · live · one step to undo
you › (points at the page list) Sort these by date.
agent · intent updated, checks still pass · preview ready

No app store browsing, no settings archaeology, no terminal required. The agent works inside limits you set, builds each change in a private copy, shows you a preview, and makes it live when you confirm. You can undo one change and keep the later ones. Anything that leaves your machines, spends money, or acts as you needs your say.

Seven first principles

01

Intent, checks, and the pinned build are the source

Your intents, the checks you confirmed, and the builds you accepted are your OS. Checks are scenarios in a fixed format, worded by templates and not by a model. A better model can re-derive a Tool only if the checks pass and a replay matches.

02

Layers with their own undo

Base changes rarely, at boot. The Environment changes one Tool at a time: fork, preview, promote. Your Data is never rolled back with them, and undoing software never restores a grant you revoked.

03

Deterministic security, first

Handles, effect limits, and a gate with no model in it decide what can happen. Private values stay out of the agent's context, and nothing a model does cleans a stranger's text. Anything that crosses the undo boundary needs a grant and a rule, or you, and nothing but you can say yes.

04

Tools are living and disposable

A Tool starts with nothing and gets everything through the broker, whether it is WASM or native. The system draws its UI. Point at any part and say what to change.

05

The agent is a role

The OS owns the role and its authority. The harness and model are swappable, from open weights on your own hardware to Grok Bot, and none of them can allow anything.

06

The owner is sovereign

You can always take full control. Lean by default, as heavy as you choose. Recovery never depends on a model.

07

Portable and shareable

One OS across your fleet, with encrypted sync. Share a Tool as its pinned build with its intent and checks; a friend's OS verifies it, and no authority transfers.

The stack: invent the top, borrow the bottom

Nothing about agent-first needs a new way to talk to a Wi-Fi chip, and drivers are what kill new operating systems. We choose Linux for its drivers. The capability broker decides what each Tool may reach, and the Linux sandbox enforces it.

Already working: Milestone 1, a Void Linux VM on Btrfs where every apply is a bootable generation and a broken change is undone from the boot menu. Next: the security rules built on stock Linux and attacked. We try to make it leak a private value and act as you, and we count how much ordinary work still gets done. Then Base generations built from a complete pinned package set.

The hard problems still open

  1. Too much asking. Previews, releases, and asks all end at you. How many a day before you stop reading them?
  2. The plan language. How much can an agent do through handles before it needs to see a value it may not see?
  3. Checks for everyone. Can people who aren't developers say what they rely on in a fixed scenario format?
  4. Marks spread. One file from outside marks what touches it. How much of your Data ends up marked?
  5. Admission. Who reviews the dependency set that build tasks are allowed to read?
  6. Loss and recovery. A lost machine, a lost vault, a lost lead machine.
